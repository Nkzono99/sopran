    ER Model Definitions¶

    This page defines the model, objective, numerics, and quality checks of the two estimators used in Electron Reflectometry. Both take ElectronReflectionCounts over energy × folded pitch angle (affected and reference counts and exposures).

    finite-bin model¶

    from sopran.experimental.electron_reflection import (
    FiniteBinFitSettings, FiniteBinObservation, fit_finite_bin_distribution, profile_mirror_ratio,
)

observation = FiniteBinObservation.from_counts(
    counts, energy_edges_eV=energy_edges, pitch_edges_deg=folded_pitch_edges,
)
result = fit_finite_bin_distribution(observation, settings=FiniteBinFitSettings())
profile = profile_mirror_ratio(observation, result)

    Model equation¶

    The incident flux, integrated over solid angle, is multiplied by the reflectance and a beam, moved by the angular transport \(D_\mathrm{out}\), and mapped back to observed ratios through ideal bin responses uniform in log energy and pitch angle.

    \[ N_\mathrm{out} = D_\mathrm{out}\left\{ s\left[\rho + (1-\rho)P_0\right] N_\mathrm{in} + N_\mathrm{beam} \right\}, \qquad \sin^2\alpha_c = \frac{1 + \Delta U / E}{R_m}. \]
    Symbol Name in result.parameters Meaning
    \(R_m\) mirror_ratio Magnetic mirror ratio; \(B_\mathrm{eff} = R_m B_\mathrm{sc}\) (result.effective_field_nT)
    \(\Delta U\) delta_u_eV Potential difference, surface minus spacecraft (eV)
    \(\rho\) bottom_ratio Relative level inside the loss cone; inside is \(s\rho\), outside \(s\)
    \(s\) scale Overall scale
    sigma_deg Diffusion strength of \(D_\mathrm{out}\)
    \(\phi\) concentration Beta-binomial concentration
    \(\alpha_c\) Pitch angle of the loss-cone boundary
    \(P_0\) Reflection indicator: 1 outside the boundary, 0 inside

    Search ranges are set with mirror_ratio_bounds, delta_u_bounds_eV, bottom_ratio_bounds, scale_bounds, sigma_bounds_deg, and concentration_bounds in FiniteBinFitSettings.

    The beam is enabled by default and disabled with beam_enabled=False. There are four shapes combining log-energy widths 0.15 and 0.30 with pitch widths 10° and 20°. The amplitude is chosen continuously within beam_amplitude_bounds (default 0.25–8) by a one-dimensional golden-section search per shape at every evaluation. By default (beam_energy="delta_u") the centre energy is \(-\Delta U\) and the beam is active only for \(\Delta U < 0\), following Halekas et al. (2008, 2011) for secondary electrons accelerated from a negatively charged surface. With beam_energy="free", the centre is fitted within beam_energy_bounds_eV (default 20–200 eV) as a seventh parameter (FiniteBinParameters.beam_energy_eV, starting at 45 eV), and the beam is active whatever the sign of \(\Delta U\), so that a low-energy excess unrelated to \(\Delta U\) does not produce a spurious negative \(\Delta U\). In results, beam_template is 0 for no beam, otherwise 1 + 2*energy_index + pitch_index; the amplitude is beam_amplitude and the centre beam_energy_eV (\(-\Delta U\) for delta_u).

    A known efficiency ratio is added to the model ratio as the observation's model_offset_dex (log10 of the affected/reference efficiency ratio). scale_prior_sigma_dex places a zero-centered Gaussian prior on \(\log_{10}s\) (count losses only). Together with the offset, it moves the prior center to a calibration value.

    Angular transport \(D_\mathrm{out}\)¶

    Enable it with FiniteBinFitSettings(angular_transport="out"); the default "none" is diagonal (no transport).

    result.d_out[j_out, j_in] is the probability of moving from a source bin to a destination bin, and each column sums to 1. Its axis edges are result.transport_pitch_edges_deg; use 100 * result.d_out to display percentages. It acts after reflection and the beam and never mixes energies. It is axisymmetric diffusion with no-flux boundaries at 0° and 90° of the folded hemisphere, which keeps an isotropic flux stationary. sigma_deg is a diffusion parameter that matches the RMS direction change in the small-angle limit; for large values it is not the simple Gaussian standard deviation of pitch-angle differences. Zero reproduces the diagonal case.

    Objective¶

    loss Observable Use
    "beta_binomial" (default) Affected counts given affected + reference trials Main analysis; systematic overdispersion is absorbed by the concentration \(\phi\)
    "binomial" Same Comparison assuming statistical errors only
    "huber" (default 0.1 dex) \(\log_{10}(a/r)\) Flux-only inputs, comparison with earlier analyses
    "squared" \(\log_{10}(a/r)\) Comparison

    Count losses treat the affected count \(a\) as binomial with \(n = a + r\) trials. The affected probability is \(q = \lambda/(1+\lambda)\) with \(\lambda = x\,10^{c}R\), where \(R\) is the model ratio, \(x\) the affected/reference exposure ratio, and \(c\) model_offset_dex. The beta-binomial has mean \(q\) and concentration \(\phi\), estimated jointly with the other parameters. objective_sum is the \(-2\log L\) difference from the saturated binomial likelihood (the deviance), so differences between two fits read on a χ² scale. Count losses need an observation with counts (created with from_counts).

    The Huber objective of a residual \(r\) is \(r^2\) for \(|r| \le 0.1\) and \(0.2|r| - 0.01\) otherwise. objective_sum and the per-cell objective_mean are returned. root_mean_square_error_dex is computed separately from squared residuals and is distinct from the objective value.

    Cells used in the fit¶

    from_counts defaults to selection="total" and min_counts=1, keeping cells with \(a + r \ge 1\). Cells with \(a = 0\) enter the objective as information from inside the loss cone. selection="paired", min_counts=5 is the earlier selection that requires at least five counts on both sides. Because it selects on affected counts, cells deep in the loss cone drop out and \(\rho\) is biased upward. On synthetic counts with true \(\rho = 0.15\), paired + Huber gave 0.18–0.6 and total + beta-binomial gave 0.15.

    With either selection, only energies with at least three pitch angles and exposure ratios between 0.01 and 100 are fitted. Log ratios use a pseudocount of 0.5 and serve RMSE and figures. Instrument calibration and potential corrections happen before this fit, and the incident flux keeps relative values across pitch angles within each energy.

    Missing incident bins are interpolated in log flux within the same energy, with the nearest value at the ends. Interpolated bins are returned in interpolated_incident_bins, and the missing cells themselves are not added to the objective. Interpolated incident flux flows into observed cells through \(D_\mathrm{out}\), so many gaps make input assumptions matter. Reconstructions for unused energy rows stay NaN.

    Numerics¶

    Reflection boundaries and beams are evaluated with composite 17-point Gauss quadrature, and each pitch bin is split into 8 by default. The search uses seeded differential evolution (35 candidates, 180 generations, 2 seeds) and bounded Nelder–Mead (up to 650 iterations). When starts is omitted, Nelder–Mead also starts from 12 points: \(R_m\) = 1.2, 2, 5, 20 and \(\Delta U\) = −50, 0, +50 eV, because differential evolution alone missed the best minimum in 2 of 7 KAGUYA windows. Everything from the response to the optimization runs in Rust, with no Python callbacks and no implicit fallback to Python.

    Quality checks¶

    local_converged reports local solver termination, and at_bounds that a free parameter sits at its search bound. field_unconstrained is True when any of the following field_flags is set.

    Flag Condition
    no_contrast \(\rho \ge 0.99\)
    barrier_unobserved All fitted cells lie on one side of the boundary
    field_at_search_bound \(R_m\) at its search bound
    boundary_rows_insufficient Fewer energy rows with cells on both sides of the boundary than min_boundary_rows (default 3)
    loss_cone_unsupported loss_cone_delta_bic ≤ 0 with a count loss

    loss_cone_delta_objective is the objective difference between two models that allow free levels per energy row: no loss cone (\(\rho = 1\)) and the fitted loss cone, with the same beam. A step only in energy is explained by both, so this difference measures whether pitch-angle structure is needed. loss_cone_delta_bic subtracts \(k\ln N\), where \(k\) is the number of free parameters among \(R_m\), \(\rho\), and \(\Delta U\). On real data a certain fraction of windows is loss_cone_unsupported, and the fraction is higher for solutions with \(R_m \le 1\).

    Profile intervals¶

    No flags is not proof of identifiability, so adopt field values through profile_mirror_ratio. It fixes \(R_m\) on a grid (default ±1 dex, 21 points) and re-optimizes the other parameters from nearby solutions. With count losses, \(\Delta\)objective ≤ 3.84 defines the 95% interval, with ends interpolated between grid points.

    Result Meaning
    interval_nT 95% interval of \(B_\mathrm{eff}\)
    interval_bounded=False The interval reached the edge of the grid
    improved_minimum=True The fit missed a better minimum, returned as minimum_fit

    Results of evaluate with fixed parameters have local_converged False.

    Fixing parameters¶

    Equal lower and upper bounds fix a parameter. For example, to fix \(B_\mathrm{eff}\) to a regional mean field and refit \(\Delta U\) and the rest, use mirror_ratio_bounds=(area_mean_nT / b_sc, area_mean_nT / b_sc). bottom_ratio_bounds=(0.1, 0.1) and scale_bounds=(1, 1) compare finite-bin at the fixed 0.1/1 levels.

    Halekas model¶

    from sopran.experimental.electron_reflection import HalekasFitSettings, fit_halekas_distribution

result = fit_halekas_distribution(counts, settings=HalekasFitSettings())

    Halekas minimizes squared residuals of exposure-corrected natural-log ratios. Cells with \(a + r\) below min_cell_total_counts (default 1) are dropped, because empty cells get a ratio of 1 from the pseudocount and would look like the loss cone is absent. root_mean_square_error is in natural-log units; divide by np.log(10) before comparing with a dex RMSE on the same input and mask.

    edge_transition Model and search
    "hard" (default) Binary distribution with fixed backscatter (default 0.1) and outer level 1, evaluated at bin centers; grid search and refinement over \(R_m\) and \(\Delta U\)
    "probit" Same levels with a boundary width; L-BFGS-B starting from the hard solution and others

    Free bottom, scale, beams, and \(D_\mathrm{out}\) belong to finite-bin. The probit width (boundary smoothing) and \(D_\mathrm{out}\) (transport between angles) are different parameters. When comparing RMSE, match units and masks, and do not compare the Halekas Gaussian BIC with Huber values directly.

    For comparison: the robust mirror-transmission model¶

    from sopran.experimental.electron_reflection.mirror_transmission import (
    MirrorTransmissionSettings, fit_mirror_transmission, fit_mirror_transmission_variants, ratio_from_counts,
)

energy, pitch, ratio, b_sc = ratio_from_counts(counts)      # affected/reference rate ratio
fit = fit_mirror_transmission(energy, pitch, ratio, b_sc, settings=MirrorTransmissionSettings())
variants = fit_mirror_transmission_variants(energy, pitch, ratio, b_sc)   # four nested models

    A comparison estimator ported from lunarsat (robust_mirror_transmission_log_ratio_v1). The natural log of the ratio is modelled as \(a_j + A_j\,P(E_j,\alpha_k)\) with per-energy baseline \(a_j\) and contrast \(A_j\) nuisance terms; only the boundary and width shared across energy are estimated.

    \[ \sin^2\alpha_c = p_0\left(1 + \frac{\Delta U + \beta_1 s^2 + \beta_2 s^3}{E - U_\mathrm{sc}}\right),\quad s = \frac{E_*}{E - U_\mathrm{sc} + E_*},\qquad P = \Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln\sin^2\alpha - \ln\sin^2\alpha_c}{\sigma(E)}\right) \]
    Symbol Name Meaning
    \(p_0\) p0 High-energy asymptote of \(B_\mathrm{sc}/B_\mathrm{eff}\); b_eff_nT = b_sc_reference_nT / p0
    \(\Delta U\) delta_u_eff_eV Potential term (fixed to 0 in mirror_only)
    \(\beta_1, \beta_2\) smooth_beta1_eV, smooth_beta2_eV Low-energy correction of smooth_low_energy; the high-energy limit is unchanged
    \(\sigma\) sigma_ln_b Log-width of the boundary; energy_broadened uses \(\sqrt{\sigma^2 + (U_w/(E+E_0))^2}\)

    The loss is soft_l1 (robust_f_scale = 0.2) with scipy.optimize.least_squares. fit.success requires both optimiser convergence and a boundary inside \(0<\sin^2\alpha_c<1\); compare variants through the difference from no_edge_rmse_log (row means only) and through aic_log/bic_log. Relative to the lunarsat version only the initial guess (per-row threshold crossings) is simplified; model, bounds and loss are the same. Its input and objective differ from the finite-bin count likelihood, so the numbers are not compared directly.
