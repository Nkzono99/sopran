    PACE (Electrons And Ions)¶

    PACE (Plasma energy Angle and Composition Experiment) is the set of four particle analyzers on KAGUYA. SOPRAN reads the public PBF1 (v3.0) files and uses the same variable names for all four.

    Entry point Sensor Particles
    kg.esa1 ESA-S1 Electrons
    kg.esa2 ESA-S2 Electrons
    kg.ima IMA Ions (mass analysis)
    kg.iea IEA Ions

    Variables¶

    Variable Dimensions Units Meaning
    counts time, energy, look count Raw counts
    energy_flux time, energy, look eV/(cm^2 s sr eV) Differential energy flux computed from counts with the calibration tables
    energy energy channel / eV Energy channel index when uncalibrated, energy center in eV when calibrated
    quality time flag Record quality flag

    look is the index of a detector viewing direction, not the direction itself. Directions (polar and azimuth angles) require the calibration tables; the pitch-angle spectra below use them. energy_flux has the aliases eflux and differential_energy_flux.

    Loading¶

    kg = spn.kaguya
time = spn.day("2008-01-01")

flux = kg.esa1.energy_flux.load(time)       # SopranArray
esa1 = kg.esa1.load(time)                   # instrument-level result

esa1.counts, esa1.energy_flux, esa1.quality
esa1.to_xarray()                            # xarray.Dataset with all variables
esa1.to_polars("counts", reduce_look="sum") # table summed over look
esa1.files                                  # source files
esa1.pace                                   # decoded headers and records

    To decode source files directly, use sopran.missions.kaguya.read_pace_pbf(files). Decoding runs in the Rust extension and falls back to the Python reference implementation when the extension is missing (backend="auto").

    Energy flux calibration¶

    energy_flux is computed from counts as

    \[ \mathrm{energy\_flux} = \frac{\mathrm{counts}}{t_\mathrm{integ}\; G\; \varepsilon} \]

    where \(t_\mathrm{integ}\) is the integration time, \(G\) the g-factor from the PACE INFO table, and \(\varepsilon\) the detection efficiency. The default \(\varepsilon = 0.6\) is the uniform value used by SPEDAS kgy_esa1_get3d.pro and kgy_esa2_get3d.pro. For another value, call kg.esa1.load(time).to_energy_flux(efficiency=...).

    The calibration tables (INFO and FOV) load automatically. They come from the same distribution as SPEDAS kgy_read_inf.pro and kgy_read_fov.pro, which is served over HTTP only. Retrieved tables are recorded in the Store's source-file registry with their URL and sha256. Without the tables, loading energy_flux raises; uncalibrated values are never returned under that name. After calibration the energy coordinate holds energy centers in eV, and the original channel index stays in the energy_channel coordinate.

    cal = kg.esa1.load_calibration(download="never")   # read the tables explicitly
esa1 = kg.esa1.load(time, calibration=cal)

    All four sensors use the same formula and table handling. Regression tests cover ESA1; the flux of ESA2, IMA, and IEA has not been compared numerically with SPEDAS yet.

    Missing value 65535¶

    PBF1 counts are 16-bit unsigned integers. Following the SPEDAS PACE readers (kgy_read_pbf.pro, kgy_esa1_get3d.pro), SOPRAN converts the maximum value 65535 to NaN as missing. The PDS3 labels we checked declare no missing value such as MISSING_CONSTANT. Zero counts stay zero.

    Pitch-angle spectra¶

    pitch_angle_spectrum() turns look back into directions with the calibration tables and bins by the angle to the magnetic field, producing a time × energy × pitch_angle array.

    from sopran.missions.kaguya import selene_spice_kernels

b = kg.lmag.magnetic_field.load(time)                         # MOON_ME
frames = spn.FrameContext(spice_kernels=selene_spice_kernels(kg.store, time))   # fetch Moon and attitude kernels

pas = kg.esa1.energy_flux.pitch_angle_spectrum(
    time,
    magnetic_field=b,
    frame_context=frames,             # rotate the MOON_ME field into the PACE viewing frame
    pitch_bins="native",
    cache="use",
)
pas.plot()                                          # pitch vs time and energy vs time
pas.energy_spectrogram(pitch=(0.0, 30.0), log_color=True)

    A fixed vector in the viewing frame, such as magnetic_field=[1.0, 0.0, 0.0], works without kernels for quick tests.

    Argument Meaning
    magnetic_field Required. A vector in the viewing frame, or a SopranArray that carries its frame
    look_frame, magnetic_frame Frames of the viewing directions (default SELENE_M_SPACECRAFT) and the field. When they differ, pass a FrameContext as frame_context
    pitch_bins "native" (16 bins for 4×16-direction records, 32 for 16×64; the larger on mixed days), a bin count, or bin edges
    min_look_bins Minimum number of viewing directions per pitch-angle bin
    count_correction "none" (default), "event", "trash", "event_trash"; corresponds to SPEDAS /cntcorr
    cache With "use", results with the same arguments are saved in the features layer and reused

    count_correction="trash" redistributes discarded counts (trash counters) over azimuth, and "event" scales adjacent energy pairs to the event counter. The correction is kept as a separate multiplier, so raw counts remain integers.

    Results carry the per-record exposure and the coordinates pace_data_mode, pace_data_type, pace_submode, pace_svs_tbl, pace_data_quality, and record_duration_seconds. Whether a record may be used for pitch angles is decided from the observation mode; the rules are in PACE ESA Mode Policy.

    Ion 3-D distributions (IMA and IEA)¶

    ion_distribution() turns IMA and IEA records into 3-D distributions shaped like those of SPEDAS kgy_ima_get3d and kgy_iea_get3d, returning one PaceIon3D per record type.

    time = spn.day("2009-02-17")
iea = kg.iea.load(time, calibration=kg.iea.load_calibration()).ion_distribution(
    count_correction="event_trash"
)
d = iea[0x81]                       # 32 energy × 16 polar × 64 azimuth
d.counts                            # (record, 32, bin), bin = polar + azimuth × 16
d.to_units("eflux")                 # counts / rate / eflux / flux / df
d.density()                         # cm^-3
d.velocity()                        # km/s, SELENE_M_SPACECRAFT

from sopran.frames import FrameContext
from sopran.missions.kaguya import selene_spice_kernels

context = FrameContext(spice_kernels=tuple(map(str, selene_spice_kernels(kg.store, time))))
d.velocity(frame="MOON_ME", context=context)
d.directions(frame="MOON_ME", context=context)   # direction of motion for each bin
    Type Sensor Shape Handling
    0x40 IMA 4 polar × 32 energy × 1024 TOF TOF summed; no azimuth (dphi = 360°); gfactor of azimuth channel 4
    0x41 IMA 32 × 16 × 64
    0x42 IMA 32 × 4 × 16
    0x43 IMA 8 mass × 32 × 4 × 16 mass channels summed
    0x80 IEA 32 × 4 × 16
    0x81 IEA 32 × 16 × 64

    Other types (IMA 0x44 and 0x45, IEA 0x82) are not handled by SPEDAS either; the skipped counts are listed in notes.

    • Energies, angles and geometric factors come from the INFO tables, placed by the energy and polar sequence numbers (angles="fov" uses the FOV tables). grids holds one grid per RAM table and grid_index maps records to it.
    • theta_deg and phi_deg give the direction of particle motion. In the solar wind on 2009-02-17, 99.7% of the IEA speed pointed anti-sunward after conversion to MOON_ME.
    • IMA mass channels are summed and treated as protons, as in SPEDAS.
    • Units follow kgy_pace_convert_units and moments kgy_n_3d and kgy_j_3d, with efficiency 0.6 (the SPEDAS placeholder). No dead-time correction is applied.
    • Count correction is the SPEDAS /cntcorr. IMA uses the sum of the three TOF event rows, and type 0x40 has no trash telemetry, so only the event step is applied.
    • Differences from SPEDAS: cells whose mass channels are all fill values stay NaN (SPEDAS gives 0), and the energy widths denergy_eV are absolute values.

    Checked on 2009-02-17 (solar wind): IEA speed was 0.99–1.00 times OMNI and density 0.46–0.70 times. On the dayside (SZA < 45°) the IMA flux moving away from the Moon was about 0.7–0.9% of the solar-wind flux (OMNI nv).

    Saving to Parquet¶

    record = (
    kg.esa1.energy_flux.pipeline(spn.period("2008-01-01", "2008-01-03"))
    .calibrate(calibration="auto")
    .write("kaguya.esa1.energy_flux", layer="normalized", partition="day")
    .run()
)

    To save counts, start from kg.esa1.counts.pipeline(...) and drop calibrate. Running pipelines is covered in Batch Pipelines.

    Limitations¶

    • Saved Parquet does not yet include look directions (polar and azimuth angles) or energy coordinate metadata.
    • ESA2, IMA, and IEA energy flux has not been compared numerically with SPEDAS.
    • ion_distribution() densities use efficiency 0.6; IEA solar-wind density is 30–50% below OMNI. IMA TOF mass separation (kgy_read_tof) is not implemented yet.
    • The bit meanings of data_quality are not settled in public documents, so the value is kept as is in pace_data_quality.
